## The brief

For six years I was lead instructor for Georgia Tech's full-stack flex program,
running cohorts of fifteen-plus students at a time. The harder, more interesting job
came later: curriculum engineer on a full overhaul, treating the curriculum as a
system with inputs, feedback, and outcomes rather than a static syllabus.

## What I built

The overhaul modernized the whole stack a student moves through. It added AI and
LangChain, moved core material onto TypeScript, introduced CI/CD and test-driven
development as habits rather than topics, and added a Python module. The goal was a
curriculum that compiles, in the sense that the path from day one to a shippable
project is tested, current, and repeatable across instructors and cohorts.

## The scale

The curriculum ran across the university boot-camp network delivered through edX and
2U, so the same work reached programs at institutions such as SMU and UPenn. That
multi-institution reach is the point: this was not one classroom, it was a learning
system used at scale, designed to keep working when many different people run it.
The institution names here are factual attribution; the curriculum itself is theirs,
which is why this study is told in diagrams and description rather than course
material.
