## The brief

For six years I was lead instructor for Georgia Tech's full-stack flex program, running
cohorts of fifteen-plus. The more interesting job came later: curriculum engineer on a
full overhaul, treating the curriculum as a system with inputs, feedback, and outcomes
rather than a fixed syllabus.

## What I built

I modernized the whole stack a student moves through. Added AI and LangChain, moved
core material to TypeScript, made CI/CD and test-driven development habits instead of
topics, and added a Python module. The goal: a path from day one to a shippable project
that is tested, current, and repeatable across instructors and cohorts.

## The scale

It ran across the university boot-camp network through edX and 2U, so the same work
reached programs at institutions like SMU and UPenn. That reach is the point. This was
not one classroom, it was a learning system built to keep working when many different
people run it. The institution names are factual attribution; the curriculum is theirs,
which is why I tell this one in diagrams and description, not course material.
